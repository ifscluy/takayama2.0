Takayama spent his life in seclusion, sharpening his mind around a single conviction: that magitech did not need magic to become a real and equal force. His foundation was built on medicine — understanding, sustaining, and expanding the human body through magitech implants. From medicine, he moved into engineering; from engineering, into a distinct architecture, alien by Meranthe's standards.

His mission has always been the non-magi population — ordinary people who bear a disproportionate weight of conflicts they never chose. Takayama believes, with near-clinical clarity, that progress is the answer.

He does not go unarmed. The weapon he carries is his own invention — something that did not exist before he built it. It is a large rifle, dark and pearlescent, with a silhouette that feels displaced from any known era. Energy coils run partially exposed along its body, and when active, a low mechanical rhythm pulses through it — rotating pistons drawing power from its wielder's mana, steady and deliberate, like a heartbeat announcing its presence. It was designed not only to destroy, but to command. The rifle serves as a mother unit: upon activation, it deploys a network of smaller autonomous drones — compact, silent, purpose-built for threat response. They extend his reach into spaces he cannot occupy, and they answer to nothing else.
